Original assignment context: paths, benefits, and course outcomes

This reference summarizes the original CP2 assignments supplied by Rich. The learning benefits below explain the assignment design; they are not claims of measured results. The storyboard narration remains proposed wording.

What each path offers and requires
Focus Guided AI path Non-AI research path
Student benefit Generate starting possibilities, then practice deciding whether they are real, accurate, and useful to the inquiry. The student chooses the community and evaluates the candidates. Learn where the community’s conversations happen and how to enter them. Begin broadly, follow sources, notice patterns, and narrow the search through reading.
Each of 10–15 terms includes The term, a definition in the student’s own words, the AI tool that suggested it, and the non-AI source used to verify it. The term, a definition in the student’s own words, and the source where the term was found being used.
Evidence rule Every term requires independent verification. Another AI answer does not count. Replace unsupported terms. Students must not ask AI to rewrite definitions. No separate verification source is required: finding the term in authentic community discourse is already evidence. Students still identify that source and explain the meaning themselves.
Shared learning Identify a specific, defensible discourse community; select useful specialized vocabulary; understand and explain its meaning; document the research; and refine the research question. Terms must help the inquiry rather than merely relate to the topic.
Instructor benefit The common deliverables and five-point rubric provide comparable evidence of learning across both routes, while path-specific documentation makes the research process visible.

Connection to ENG 1320 outcomes

  • SLO 1 / Communication: Recognize and explain a community’s specialized language through vocabulary and definitions.
  • SLO 2 / Critical thinking: Investigate the community, evaluate evidence, and refine an inquiry.
  • SLO 3 / Communication: Interpret terminology and accurately express its meaning in the student’s own language.
  • SLO 4 / Personal responsibility: Distinguish verified research from generated suggestions and document where information came from.

Shared submission and clarity

Both paths earn the same five points and require a community paragraph, 10–15 documented terms, and an updated question, submitted as a PDF. Students may identify a changed topic or artifact at the beginning. The updated question should reflect new understanding; it does not need added jargon.

Concise explanation for the video: AI helps students find possibilities to test. Non-AI research helps students discover the conversation directly. Both require students to judge usefulness, explain meaning, document evidence, and improve their question.
